The study found no link between unsociability and negative outcomes like loneliness or depression. In fact, people who preferred solitude often felt as satisfied with life as those who were more social. They weren’t deprived or suffering; they just recharged and reflected differently. The answer isn’t just about personality; it’s also about the brain.
