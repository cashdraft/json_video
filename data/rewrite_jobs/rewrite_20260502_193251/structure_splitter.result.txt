    "text": "What helps when overthinking becomes a habit? Research suggests a shift in strategy: instead of fighting your thoughts, try changing how you see them. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, or ACT, is built on this idea. The goal isn’t to get rid of hard thoughts or argue with them, but to notice them, let them be, and focus on actions that matter to you. One key technique in ACT is called cognitive diffusion. When a thought comes up—like, “I always make mistakes”—the usual reaction is to believe it or try to push it away. Diffusion means seeing the thought as just a thought: “I’m having the thought that I always make mistakes.” Or, “I notice I’m having the thought that I always make mistakes.” This creates some distance, so the thought has less power. ACT doesn’t try to erase thoughts, but helps people notice them, accept them, and act based on their values—not just on what pops into their mind. Over time, this changes the relationship to thinking. Thoughts become background noise—sometimes helpful, sometimes not, but always just thoughts. This doesn’t stop overthinking right away. But it gives a practical way to step back from the cycle, even if it keeps going. You learn to watch your mind’s activity without getting pulled in."
